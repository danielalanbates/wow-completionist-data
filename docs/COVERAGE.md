## Cross-checks

* Retail: where the wiki gives an end point and Blizzard's turn-in pin is a single point (QuestPOIBlob with one QuestPOIPoint: the client's own turn-in marker, not an area), the two were compared in world coordinates for 7,286 quest records (faction variants included); 1,011 (13.9%) were more than 50 yd apart, and for those the pin is shipped and the wiki point is kept under `coord_source.replaces`. Whole zones can be off (The Ringing Deeps: most wiki points sit about 4 map units east of the pins while the map itself is unchanged between builds 12.1.0 and 12.1.5), so a stale wiki number is the usual cause.
* Coordinate reading, NPC / object page rows with a Blizzard position to compare (retail turn-in pins, flight points): 2,448 untagged rows; retail-map numbers 1,210, Classic-map numbers 26, either (the maps agree there) 245, more than 50 yd off both ways 248, no position inside the row's zone 719. Zones whose unchecked rows are read as Classic numbers: arathihighlands, dunmorogh.

## What the gaps mean

* The Warcraft Wiki is retail-first, and many pages still carry numbers read off the pre-Cataclysm map. Each untagged row is read on the map a Blizzard position for the same NPC agrees with; without one, by its zone's evidence (`read_by` `zone-default`), and the point is `confidence` low. On Classic clients a point carried over from a retail page is right only if the NPC did not move in Cataclysm or later; nothing shippable says which NPCs moved, so those points are medium at best. Era, TBC and Forever have no Blizzard quest coordinates to fall back on, so their located share is what the wiki gives.
* Retail ends and objectives fall back to Blizzard's quest POI (QuestPOIBlob) when the wiki has no point. Retail starts never use the POI: the turn-in pin is not the quest giver.
* MoP Classic's QuestPOIBlob carries no quest rows, so MoP has no DB2 fallback either.
* `QuestV2 ids with no wiki quest page` are mostly hidden tracking quests; the rest are real quests nobody has written a wiki page for yet. A wiki page (or a correction here) adds them.
